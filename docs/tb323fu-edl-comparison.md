# TB323FU EDL and boot-guide comparison for NX809J

Reviewed 2026-10-19 against the pinned TB323FU guide and LTBox source snapshots in [`research/tb323fu-reference/`](../research/tb323fu-reference/README.md), plus the NX809J readback and boot-chain records. This is a source comparison only: no phone, EDL session, firmware, programmer, or boot image was operated.

## Decision

**Do not apply the TB323FU rooting, EDL loader, EFISP, partition-layout, or boot-switch steps to the RedMagic 11 Pro (NX809J).** The devices share the SM8850 chipset family, but the guide is for a Lenovo Legion Tab Gen 5, model TB323FU, board Baldur, and reports checks on one ROW tablet running ZUI 18.0.12.104. It does not establish RedMagic board, bootloader, programmer, firmware, partition-map, or image-policy compatibility. The guide itself limits its evidence to that tablet and says other firmware is unchecked. [Pinned TB323FU rooting guide](https://github.com/joonhoekim/tb323fu-linux/blob/37ada75998cedee2f95007248e71879aca996739/docs/rooting.md)

That rooting procedure is specifically for an initially locked tablet with an empty `efisp`; LTBox checks the TB ABL, writes a Lenovo-specific GBL to `efisp`, and modifies `init_boot_a`. NX809J is already rooted and its bootloader currently reports `unlocked:yes`; the saved NX `efisp.img` is nonempty (SHA-256 `4df183594a63af28c681e48b1d9fdb0305ee055ea8fde92118a1e0af7a5f6f17`). The TB guide's empty-`efisp` precondition is therefore not met. The phone's observed Fastboot state and recovery constraints are recorded in [recovery.md](recovery.md) and [boot-chain.md](boot-chain.md).

LTBox source also describes explicit per-model capability profiles and cautions that its generic fallback does not authorize operations on unknown physical devices. Its profile list covers Lenovo models and does not list NX809J. [Pinned LTBox model capabilities](https://github.com/miner7222/LTBox/blob/d8bba38169a03c1b867ed1549fc75dbc6eba5cc3/crates/ltbox-core/src/model/capabilities.rs)

## What the EDL backup method can teach us

The transferable part is the **backup discipline**, not the Lenovo loader or write procedure: obtain the partition map from the exact device, read and preserve device-specific partitions, compare captured lengths with that device's own partition geometry, hash the results, and keep a separate copy. The TB guide's dump covered LUNs 0–5, included its 20 GiB `super`, excluded its own `userdata`, and checked file sizes against that tablet's GPT. Those LUN numbers, sizes, partition lists, and package contents are TB-specific. [Pinned TB323FU dump procedure](https://github.com/joonhoekim/tb323fu-linux/blob/37ada75998cedee2f95007248e71879aca996739/docs/rooting.md#L261-L286)

A future NX809J EDL read would first need a programmer the NX boot ROM accepts for this exact hardware/firmware, any required Sahara multi-image manifest and companion images, the correct authorization/authentication state, and a verified NX UFS LUN/GPT map. The TB package's loader is not a generic one-file programmer: its guide describes `qsahara_device_programmer.x` plus eight manifest-listed images, and LTBox has TB/Lenovo-specific loader and LUN handling. The TB author's statement that its loader is model-matched rather than firmware/region-matched is evidence about that loader and tablet only. [Pinned LTBox Sahara manifest parser](https://github.com/miner7222/LTBox/blob/d8bba38169a03c1b867ed1549fc75dbc6eba5cc3/crates/ltbox-core/src/sahara_xml.rs) · [LTBox partition/LUN map](https://github.com/miner7222/LTBox/blob/d8bba38169a03c1b867ed1549fc75dbc6eba5cc3/crates/ltbox-core/src/partition_lun.rs)

The project already retains **ZTE Family Toolbox 1.2.9-beta1 with an explicit NX809J profile and eight-image Kaanapali programmer bundle**. Its acceptance on this handset and complete restore coverage remain unverified; see [the static toolbox assessment](zte-toolbox-assessment.md). A complete OEM restore package has not been established. The saved NX readback has 91 verified partition images totaling 2,219,343,872 bytes, with all six primary/backup GPT pairs passing CRC checks. It does not include a raw full `super.img` or `userdata.img`; the physical `super` is about 18 GiB. A partition set of that size is valuable recovery material but is not a full firmware restore package or a personal-data backup. The approved apps/files capture is running and remains incomplete. See [recovery.md](recovery.md) and the dated backup manifests under [`backups/`](../backups/).

These gaps do not mean an NX read-only EDL dump is impossible. They mean no matching programmer or restore path has been established, so EDL accessibility and recovery cannot be promised from the TB guide. A safe-read plan would still need to verify exact target model/revision, accepted loader and companion images, transport/auth state, LUN geometry, complete partition coverage, and output hashes before it could be relied on.

## `userdata` and encryption nuance

The TB author's decision to omit that tablet's encrypted `userdata` from an image dump does not prove encrypted userdata is universally useless. Android file-based and metadata encryption keep user data encrypted at rest, with key material protected and tied to device security state. A raw same-device image may retain ciphertext useful for recovery if its metadata, relevant key state, hardware-backed services, and compatible device state remain available. It is not a plaintext backup, cannot independently recreate KeyMint/TEE secrets or biometric enrollment, and successful decryption/recovery is not guaranteed. [AOSP file-based encryption](https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/encryption/file-based) · [AOSP metadata encryption](https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/encryption/metadata)

For this phone, keep the data categories separate: the raw `userdata` partition is not in the current saved readback; the encrypted Android data backup is incomplete; and neither a ciphertext image nor ordinary app-data copies substitute for preserving device-unique boot, radio, calibration, and key material. The TB guide's partition names for such material are Lenovo-specific; follow the NX phone's own verified map instead.

## Why the TB boot switch is not the requested dual boot

On TB323FU, slot B is not a second bootable OS: its virtual-A/B `super` contains only A-side system partitions. The guide uses physical `boot_b` only to keep a hash-checked copy of the stock Android boot image. It always boots from A, writes Linux into `boot_a`, and copies the saved Android image back into `boot_a` when switching back. Thus the guide does **not** put Android and Linux in separate boot slots. [Pinned TB323FU boot-switch explanation](https://github.com/joonhoekim/tb323fu-linux/blob/37ada75998cedee2f95007248e71879aca996739/docs/rooting.md#L335-L346)

For NX809J, the current saved metadata marks A successful and B not successful with zero retries; the B-side logical partitions in `super` have zero extents. Those facts mean B is **not a known-good boot target**. They do not by themselves make the physical `boot_b` partition unusable as a place to store backup bytes. What makes the TB switch unsuitable is that it overwrites the known-good `boot_a` on every OS switch, conflicts with the requested Android-on-one-slot/Linux-on-another layout, and depends on the TB-specific persistent bootloader/AVB behavior and its backup-hash guard. Do not infer that NX accepts the same persistent image or switch process.

The TB repacker is also a different boot contract from the NX saved temporary Fastboot path. TB builds a persistent Android boot image around a raw/gzip ARM64 Linux `Image`, preserving the tablet's Android header, GKI signature block, vbmeta blob, and AVB footer for a patched Lenovo GBL/ABL path. It is not a generic repacker and will not wrap an EFI PE/UKI as the kernel payload. [Pinned TB repacker](https://github.com/joonhoekim/tb323fu-linux/blob/37ada75998cedee2f95007248e71879aca996739/tools/boot-repack-kernel.py)

The analyzed NX EFISP `CmdBoot` callback instead checks for an `ANDROID!` wrapper with an `MZ` EFI image in the kernel field, then passes only those PE bytes to UEFI `LoadImage`/`StartImage`; it does not forward the Android ramdisk or command line. That is static analysis of the saved EFISP, not proof that this exact image served the recent USB Fastboot session, that a PE image will be accepted, or that Linux will boot. The NX callback and its limits are documented in [boot-chain.md](boot-chain.md). A temporary Fastboot wrapper is not the same thing as persistently replacing a slot image while preserving AVB metadata.

## What is safe to reuse now

- Preserve and hash the NX809J's own existing verified readbacks and GPT captures; keep Android A as the known working return path.
- Use the TB write-up as a checklist for what a future **NX-specific read-only backup design** must prove: exact model/firmware provenance, programmer and all companion images, all physical LUNs, GPT-derived extents, full-image hashes, and restore coverage.
- Keep boot selection, backup storage, Android userdata, and Linux root storage as distinct design choices. `boot_b` storage is a possible concept to evaluate separately, not evidence that NX slot B can boot Linux.
- Retain the TB-specific rollback, attestation, and remote-key reports as reports about TB323FU. The guide reports an update rollback limit and LTBox docs discuss consequences of a particular GBL on the locked TB323FU; neither establishes an NX809J fuse, irreversibility boundary, attestation change, or recovery behavior. [TB323FU recovery notes](https://github.com/joonhoekim/tb323fu-linux/blob/37ada75998cedee2f95007248e71879aca996739/docs/recovery.md) · [LTBox troubleshooting](https://miner7222.github.io/ltbox/en/troubleshooting.html)

No NX EDL session, programmer trial, partition write, slot change, boot-image construction, or phone modification was performed for this comparison.
